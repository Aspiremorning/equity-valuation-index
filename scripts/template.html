Methodology — How the EVI is built

Composite construction. Eleven prime factors — P/E, P/B, Market Cap to GDP in ₹ and $ terms, Earnings Yield, India 10-Year G-Sec, BEER Ratio, 91-Day T-Bill, PREITY Ratio, Yield Gap, and the India–US 10-Year spread — are each converted to a percentile rank against their full daily history since March 2003.

Direction adjustment. Factors where a higher reading signals cheapness (Earnings Yield, and the India–US 10-Year spread — a wider sovereign risk premium means more compensation on offer, i.e., cheaper; a compressed spread means risk priced for perfection) are inverted, so every factor reads on a single scale: 0 = cheapest in history, 100 = richest in history. The composite EVI is the equal-weighted average of the eleven adjusted percentiles.

Interpretation bands. 0–20 Deep Value · 20–40 Value · 40–60 Fair Value · 60–80 Expensive · 80–100 Extreme Expensive. Share of history spent in each band is shown in the legend above.

Market Cap to GDP. Both series use calendar-year-matched GDP (IMF/MoSPI table, editable in the repository); ₹-terms GDP is derived at the year-average USD/INR where an explicit ₹ figure is not supplied. PREITY = P/E × 91-Day T-Bill; BEER = 10-Year yield ÷ Earnings Yield; Yield Gap = 10-Year yield − Earnings Yield.
